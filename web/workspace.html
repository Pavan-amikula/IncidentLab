How to use this dashboard

Open Live demo. Choose “Healthy requests” first and click Start. This creates new HTTP requests; it never retrains the model.

Check the collection status and timestamps. “Running” means new observations are arriving. “Complete” means you are looking at the last saved observations.

Check Requests, p95 latency and Errors. p95 is the latency below which 95% of captured requests finished. An increase can mean slow service behavior; no captured requests can also mean a collection gap.

Compare the two alert columns. ML is Isolation Forest inference. Operational warnings also use latency/error rules and missing-request checks. They can disagree.

Read the investigation steps and trace evidence. Check the downstream service and capture completeness before claiming a cause. Run the delay demo to see latency spread upstream.

Confusion matrix: rows are evaluation labels, columns are detector decisions. Top-right = false alarms, bottom-left = missed fault windows.
ROC: detection versus false-positive rate across score thresholds. Precision–recall: how many alerts are faults versus how many faults are found. Training curves: recorded loss and validation history, not newly trained results.
RCAEval curves are separated by system. Kubernetes matrices use control-proxy window labels and carry capture caveats. No external-transfer accuracy graph is invented where healthy ground truth is unavailable.
